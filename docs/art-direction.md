# Art direction

The game should look like a hand-drawn GIF you can walk around in. It's a real 3D scene, but every frame reads as an animated drawing: chalk lines that boil, scribbled hatching, a limited palette, dithering and glitches.

`index.html` is the working implementation. `reference/stuckeys-bridge.html` is the scene the game grew out of. See "What only the reference has" below.

## How it's made

Everything so far is drawn in code with three.js, using the helpers in `index.html`:

| Helper | What it does |
|---|---|
| `makeHatch` | Scribbled crosshatch cloth, as several drawings to cycle through |
| `toonGrad`, `hoodMat` | Three-step toon shading (70, 150, 255) with the hatch as both colour and glow |
| `tapeMat` | The warning tape in the hero's hood |
| `drawPerson`, `drawRadiation`, `drawAura` | What everyone else had on, how it's going through them and what's coming off them, drawn on a canvas at load, in `src/looks.js` |
| `zombieMat` | Putting those on a body, one at a time or a whole crowd at once |
| `outlineMat`, `outline` | Screen-space outlines. A `chalk` value above 0 adds the boil and the broken chalk texture |
| `pixelTex`, `triplanar`, `textTex` | Surface textures and painted signage |
| `vehicleGeometries`, `vehMat` | Everything with an engine: low-poly boxes in `src/vehiclemodels.js`, with paint that takes each one's colour and rusts through in patches |
| `trainGeometries`, `gateArm` | The trains and the crossing arms: low-poly boxes in `src/trainmodels.js` on the vehicles' material, so each car takes its own faded colour and rusts the same way |
| `writeStructure`, `houseMat` | Every real building: `src/structures.js` writes its pieces, and one material draws the siding, brick, block, ribs, shingle, windows and doors from what's in the vertices |
| `breakable` | Anything that isn't a building and can be knocked down. While its function runs it collects the boxes, solids and kudzu it adds, and how it goes over comes from its options |
| `spray`, `dust`, `puff` | Bits off what's breaking, in a fixed pool, and the dust where it lands |
| `burnAt`, `drawFire` | Everything on fire is one list. The nearest of it is drawn on the twelve frames: low-poly flames with an ink line, a glow on the ground, smoke and sparks. None of it adds a light |
| `blast`, `thud` | Something going up, with its fireball and the fire it starts, and something heavy landing, which breaks what it lands on and stays dark |
| `swayMat` | Foliage that moves |
| `gifMat`, `renderFrame` | The final look: dither, grain, vignette and glitches |
| `triggerBurst`, `glitch` | Glitch bursts: `glitch` for hits, `triggerBurst` for the rare timed one |
| `updateParticles` | Leaves, fireflies, cyan drips and mist, wrapped around the camera |

This approach came from a failed one. Flat shapes drawn in code and set against painted frames looked like clip art and were thrown out. Building a real 3D scene and making it feel hand-drawn is what worked, so don't go back to flat sprites or paste painted images into the scene.

## The protagonist

- His proportions are measured off the coin: a big hood, 0.78 m wide and 0.9 m tall with a rounded top, sitting right down between sloping shoulders half as wide again. One continuous hood flowing into the shoulders (`HERO_SHAPE`), a little smaller all over so that it's the hood that's big.
- Black cloth `#08050a` scribbled with hot red crosshatch: `#d81f4f`, `#f2305f`, `#7e1030`.
- A chalky orange outline, `#ff7a2c`, that wobbles from frame to frame.
- A wide opening in the hood with a raised rim: arched at the top, widest just below the middle, a V at the chin.
- There's no face in there. There's warning tape, set back inside the hood (`tapeMat`): flat and straight, amber and black in equal parts, 34 degrees off vertical and rising to the right, so you see three wide bars and a sliver at each end. The bars are scribbled in with the same strokes as his hoodie, and the strokes rough up their edges. It's worked out along the line of sight, so it stays flat and slides behind the rim as he turns.
- White-outlined black drawstrings hanging long and a little wavy from white eyelets either side of the chin.
- Hanging, his head lolls away from the rope. Swimming, it comes up out of the water.
- Dark fists outlined in orange.
- A long-neck bottle with a white chalk outline (`#f6f2ea`) and glowing cyan liquid (`#3ad9d2`). The label is black with an orange warning triangle and no lettering. The liquid stays level as the bottle tips, so it runs up into the neck.

## Everyone else

Everyone else is a radiating zombie, and how far gone they are varies. That's the owner's word for it, in `docs/lore.md`. How they look is below, and the owner can overrule any of it. `data/looks.md` explains `data/looks.json`.

- **A thin person.** Narrow and stooped, with long arms and a small bare head hanging forward on its neck (`ZOMBIE_SHAPE`), so that you can tell them from him by shape alone, a long way off. They were people from around here, and they've still got on what they had on: flannel, overalls, camo with a hunter's vest, a church dress, coveralls, or no shirt. It's all in crayon rather than his scribble, with the paper's tooth in it, going dark into the outline, and it never blooms, whatever light they're standing in.
- **How far gone** is one number from 0 to 1, and everything below goes by it. `drawRadiation` draws one map of when each spot gives way, and `zombieMat` compares the map to the number, so someone at any stage costs the same as anyone else.
- **Cracks** start at the breastbone, spread out along their branches, and light up in radium green, which stands out against the magenta sky (`RADIUM`). The trunks are brighter than the tips.
- **Burning through.** Patches of what they had on go to char, with a lit edge, until there's not much but char and cracks. The face goes last.
- **Eyes** are pinpricks to start with, and fill the sockets. The mouth lights once they're well on.
- **The line** goes from dead chalk to burning. Their skin and clothes go a little greener too.
- **What's coming off them** (`drawAura`): chalk strokes straight out from the body, like a comic drawing something that radiates, redrawn on the twelve frames, and a pool of the same green on the ground. Nothing shows until they're about a quarter gone, and the strokes get longer and more of them show from there.

With reduced motion the flicker and the strokes hold still.

Coop, Rocky and Miss Bobby are the least far gone, because they can still talk, and each has clothes nobody else wears, along with Coop's cap and chain and her hat and skirt. Each of them stands a little over at the hips, as far as their look has gone: Rocky the most and Miss Bobby the least. Robby's a ghost of the same build, and not far gone at all, because he didn't die, and he stands up straight.

Toni's an astral projection, which isn't a ghost (`astralMat`). She's drawn like a picture on an old television: every so many rows of pixels aren't there, so what's behind her shows through her in stripes, and a lighter band climbs her with the rows on the twelve frames, never past where a light would bloom. Her line wavers a little sideways as it climbs. She's a big-boned ten-year-old and not a small grown-up: about 4.7 heads tall to a grown-up's 5.8, with a round head about 70% as wide as her broad shoulders, short legs, a child's face (`face: "kid"`) with round cheeks, big eyes, a button nose and a little smile, long brown hair, a green coat too big for her, down to her knees with its sleeves past her wrists, and chunky boots. She doesn't glow and nothing comes off her. The frog's in both her cupped hands at her chest. It isn't a projection: it's solid, drawn the way things are, with no light of its own, and lit by her shimmer, a little cold light from her chest that only it gets, so that it's what you see first. It breathes, blinks and shifts in her hands. With reduced motion her rows and the frog hold still. ETB2k4 is to be drawn the same way.

## The crowd

They share one body so that dozens can be on a phone's screen at once: `crowdBody`, `crowdLimb`, `drawCrowd`. Every look is on one sheet with a row each, and each person reads their own row and how far gone they are, so seven draw calls cover forty-eight of them, what's coming off them included, however many looks there are.

- They wear the looks in `data/looks.json`. How far gone each of them is comes from a number they already have, by way of `degreeOf`, and most are in the middle.
- Everything that's lit goes hotter and yellower once they've noticed something, and what's coming off them flares even on someone who's barely started, so you can read who's after you from a distance.
- Their lines thin out with distance, or a far crowd would be nothing but chalk.
- Everyone stands folded over at the waist as far as how far gone they are says, from the ones who've burnt down, folded the furthest over, to the least far gone, only a little over, so no two in a crowd stand quite alike and the same person always stands the same way. It's a standing fold, at the hips and nowhere else, the way it is in the owner's reference picture: feet flat, the legs straight, the top half leaning forward and the hips going back a little over the heels so they don't topple, which tips the legs back and takes them down a little. Their number is the angle at the hips, 180° stood up straight and 0° folded right over, and it's drawn at 90° plus half of it, so nobody's drawn further over than 90°, with their back about level, and nobody's hands or head are near the ground. Their arms hang straight down from the shoulders whatever the fold, loose, swinging a little as they walk, and their hands never touch the ground, rest on it or walk on it. Their arms are as long as their legs, so at the deepest fold the elbows give a little. Their head hangs on its neck: stood up it tips back with the mouth open, and folded over it hangs forward and down. Looking at something, anyone whose number's 90° or more comes halfway up. It eases in and out over a few steps. Sitting straightens them out, and so does lying flat after a knock, until they get up into it again. `data/folks.md` has who stands how.
- The ones who've burnt down are the furthest gone of anyone, so they're the brightest and the most charred, give off the most and are folded the furthest over, and that's what tells you they'll come for him on sight: the lowest, brightest shapes in a crowd are the ones to steer round. Nothing else marks them out.
- You can tell how someone's taking him by how they move. Someone after him leans in with their arms out, or, if their number's under 90°, stays folded and scuttles: a quick shuffle on their feet with their arms swinging, folded over the way they stand, and never on their hands. Someone keeping away goes quickly with their hands up by their head and leaning forward. Someone following stops upright with their arms down and faces him, glowing the way they do when they've noticed something. `data/folks.md` has who does what.
- People at home look the same as anyone else, and you can tell them by where they are and how they stay put. Someone driving has their lights on and sits at the wheel in the look they'd have on foot. The kids on four-wheelers are about two-thirds the size and hardly gone at all.
- Close to him, someone who's gone quiet mutters over their head now and then: one line, small, in italic Plex Mono, cream at not quite three quarters with an ink shadow, fading in and drifting up a few pixels over three seconds before it fades away. With reduced motion it's there at once and doesn't move before it fades. It's two at once at most.
- Anything over someone's head goes over their head wherever it is, folded over or not: what they mutter and how close they are to seeing through him.
- Nobody in the crowd has a name on the screen until he knows it, and how anyone is with him is never written. It's in how they move.
- While he's shambling, how close each of the nearest five is to seeing through him is a small cream bar over their head, red near the end. Anyone off the screen has theirs at its edge on their side, with an arrow. One who's seen through it glows the way anyone does who's noticed something. `data/folks.md` has the rest.
- Limbs that don't bend, swung from the shoulder and hip on the same 12 fps step as everything else. Most of them limp. When they're after him stood up, their arms come up and they lean in.
- Before one comes for him, it stops where it is and turns to face him, and what's coming off it flares for a second, past the glow of anyone after him by 0.6 (`ROUSE_FLARE` in `index.html`), with its arms hanging as they are when they're still and nothing else changing. Nothing on the screen says why: no line, no panel and no message. One that has only looked up, because there were already three, doesn't flare. It's brightness and not movement, so reduced motion keeps it. Backing off in that second sends it back to what it was doing and the glow falls with it.
- Close enough to grab him, they rear back a little with their hands coming up over their heads, for the half second it takes them to get hold. That's the moment to swing.
- The camera's behind him, so one coming straight at him is hidden by his hood for the last couple of metres, and its body can't be what warns him. What's coming off it flares as its hands go up: wider and brighter than on anyone else, up to the hot end of the radium green (`RADIUM_HOT`), until it shows round the edges of him. It groans once as the hands go up, higher and shorter than one that's just noticed him. The flare is brightness and not movement, so it's the same with reduced motion, and it's over once the swing lands or they've got hold.
- A swing that lands holds the frame they were hit in for one step and tears it, like a drawing being hit. Then they double over and go over backwards where they were thrown, in about a third of a second, and lie flat. Getting up is slow and grudging: on to their knees first, folded over and wobbling, then up into the fold. Their legs don't bend, so on their knees is lower, with the legs swept back, and they hold their arms out to the sides a little, for balance, so their hands stay off the ground. What's coming off them goes down with them. With reduced motion nothing holds or tears.
- A swing turns him to face the nearest one he can reach in front of him. The camera stays where it was.
- **The train** throws whoever it hits clear of the tracks, and they go flat where they land, the same way, with nothing to hold a frame on. They lie in the ditch a good while, 30 to 55 seconds, and get up the same slow way. With reduced motion they don't turn over on the way.
- **Flung**, by anything he's driving at 40 mph or more: they go up over the top of it or off ahead of it, limp, every joint swinging its own way on the twelve, turning over and twisting however they're going, with their glow streaking out behind them. They bounce off the road once or twice, less each time, and come down in a heap: flat on whichever of their back or front they were nearer, rolled a little onto their side, with their arms and legs splayed however they were flung but down on the ground and never through it. No two are the same. It's funny and never gory: nothing comes off anyone except, now and then, a shoe, which lands somewhere else. They lie there 16 to 20 seconds and get up the same slow way, sore. With reduced motion they fly without turning over, flailing or streaking, held in the one shape the whole way, and nobody loses a shoe.
- **The cast** are never flung. They hop out of the way of anything coming at them, half a metre up and clear of it, watch it go by, and hop back.
- **Sore**, from the train: getting up they go home with a limp. They're slower, the leg they favour hardly swings, and they're bent over a little more.
- **Soaked**, from the river: someone the cottonmouths have comes up dark, cold and heavy. The cloth goes down to about half its brightness and bluer, and there are a few pale glints where the moon would find the wet, mostly up on the shoulders and round the edges, changing on the twelve. It's part of what they have on, so it never blooms, and with reduced motion the glints hold still. It's all of it for 40 seconds and then they dry off over the next 40.
- **A cottonmouth hanging off a sleeve**: its head has hold of the cloth halfway down one arm and the rest of it hangs in a loose S. It's olive brown with dark saddles across it and a cream mouth, an ink line like everything else, and a little light of its own, the way what they have on has, so it's not a black shape in the dark. It swings a little on the twelve, and with reduced motion it only hangs. After 14 to 30 seconds it lets go, falls, lies a moment and slithers off, shrinking out of sight. With reduced motion it's just not there any more. They're one shape, an instance each, 16 at most, and no draw calls at all with none.

## The GIF feel

- **12 fps drawing over a smooth camera.** Pose, hatch and boil step at `Math.floor(time * 12)`. The camera keeps the full frame rate.
- **Boil.** Hatching cycles through three drawings (`step % 3`) and chalk outlines shift each step (`step % 4`).
- **Limited colour.** `UnrealBloomPass` (strength .5, radius .45, threshold .92), then `OutputPass`, then `gifMat`: an 8×8 Bayer dither to 30 levels per channel, grain, a vignette and slight chromatic aberration.
- **Glitches.** Scanline tears, block shifts and colour crush down to 9 levels. They respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
  - **Hits** (`glitch`) fire on the cut, the splash and a bite: big blocks that slide. These are what the trailer shows.
  - **Timed bursts** fire about every five minutes, on the owner's say: finer blocks that swap colour, and about half of them hold, freezing and smearing blocks of the previous frame. The reference fires one every 4 to 11 seconds.
  - **He stays clean.** A mask pass keeps him and his outlines out of the break-up. The mask and the hold buffers are only drawn during a burst, so calm frames cost no extra passes.
- **Particles.** Falling leaves, fireflies, mist, and a stray cyan drop from the bottle that rings the water where it lands. Phones get 140 leaves, 70 fireflies and 5 mist sprites instead of 380, 180 and 10.

## The world

Night in an abandoned Mississippi town.

- **Palette:** night `#07050b`, ink `#120b14`, cream `#f1e6cf`, rust `#ec803a`, cyan `#6fd6c8`, hot `#e3285c`, dusk `#a99bc0`.
- **Sky:** horizon `#e0662a`, clouds `#1d1230`, moonlight `#c9d4ff`, purple fog `#2a1838`.
- **Surfaces:** flat-shaded terrain and foliage, ink outlines on buildings and steel, kudzu `#3f7a35` over everything left standing. On a building it lies flat to the walls, spilling from under the eaves or climbing from the ground, and never stands off them in mounds.
- **Stuckey's Bridge:** a rusted truss with an orange rail (`#d76a2c`). It's where the real one is, 6.45 miles south-east of town and about ten by road, and the road through it is the one thing on the map that's bent, so that the opening can look straight down it.
- **The Chunky River Bridge:** where he wakes, on Griffis Fountain Rd at the edge of town. A one-lane pin-connected Pratt through truss from 1911, 30.7 m long and 4.3 m wide, in rusted steel over sand banks. The date and the size are from public descriptions, and the rest is as it looks on Street View. It has six panels and five posts a side: a plain doubled one where each leaning end post meets the top chord, and three laced ones between them, laced on the faces that look along the road. The diagonals are pairs of flat bars, crossed by a thin one only in the middle two panels. Each portal is two beams with a zigzag between them and a heavy knee down to the end post either side, and each overhead strut has a shallow brace up to its middle from each side, with thin rods crossing every panel overhead. The deck is planks laid across, ragged at the ends, with two wheel tracks of boards laid along them. A low flat rail runs just inside the posts and carries on down each side of the way in. The town sign stands just past it.
- **The town:** the real one. Every building is at its real position, with its real footprint, in siding, brick, block or ribbed metal, and with kudzu on it. See `data/structures.md`. The town hall is the small tan brick one on the west side of Commerce St, set back and looking east across its lot, the post office is across Commerce St down at US 80, the gas station's canopy stands in front of the old store it belonged to, and the church whose pantry Miss Bobby runs is the brick one at Adams and Church St. Her house is the small white one just north of the town hall, with its gable end to Commerce St, a grey door in the middle of it and a concrete step, a paved drive along its north side and the van she drives for Chunky Baptist out front. Rocky's is the cream trailer across US 80 from the gas station, its door on the side towards the road, with a plank stoop and his moccasin's tank on a stand beside the door. Real businesses go under invented names.
- **The ball field:** in the block between School St, Jones St and Adams St, built from the public aerial: orange clay, chalk, chain-link, the backstop, blue dugouts, aluminium bleachers and nine tall wooden light poles that have been dark a while. See the block in `index.html`.
- **The roads:** asphalt lighter than the ground, with patches, cracks and polished wheel paths. Double yellow down US 80 and Adams St, dashed yellow on the smaller roads, white edge lines on all of them, worn away in long stretches and speckled up close. A gravel shoulder thins out into the dirt. Paint stops short of junctions and railroad crossings. By the bridge, it's still the first scene's dark road.
- **The lanes and drives:** the named local roads are the same asphalt with no paint on them, unless they've been seen to have some, and narrower. The rest aren't paved: tan gravel, and the red clay of a Mississippi drive, each with two ruts pressed into it and an edge that wanders off into the ground. Clay has grass up the crown. A drive opens out where it leaves the road, and closes up by the door. Griffis Fountain Rd, both sides of the bridge he wakes on, is gravel with the red clay showing through.
- **The map:** drawn from the same data as the world, on a dark scratched page: cream roads with the lanes in a thinner, duller cream, a rust railroad, a teal river, dusk buildings, cyan people, everyone else playing in blue (`#5b8cff`), rust arrows. The small one turns with the camera and only appears once he's in town.
- **Vehicles:** old and rusting, in faded paint, with no real makes or badges. Cars and pickups by the houses and shops, four-wheelers by the trailers, a golf cart at every church. The van Miss Bobby drives for Chunky Baptist is a tall cream passenger van with windows all down its sides and that church's name along both in navy Bungee, off the same sheet as its sign. Their windows are open, so he's always in view at the wheel. Only the one he's driving has its lights on.
- **A prop that isn't real:** the rusted boxcar on a railroad with dark ties (`#2a1d18`).
- **Everything breaks.** Each building comes apart in the pieces it's drawn in, and when too many walls have gone the roof comes down and the rest follows. Wood burns all the way down, and brick, block, stucco and tin keep their walls and lose the roof. Fire is comic-book fire: orange and red tongues with an ink line, black smoke in lumps, no realism. An explosion is a few big hand-drawn frames on the twelve-frame clock. Dust is many small puffs, because one big one reads as a boulder. Only the two river bridges stand up to all of it, since the first scene has to stay as it is and he has to be able to wake on the other.
- **Doors stand open.** Any building can be walked into, and its roof comes off while he's inside. Anything between the camera and him opens up around him in a dither, and the roof of whatever the camera backs into comes off too.
- **Street signs:** green blades with cream Bungee, the colours of the town sign, reading the names in the road graph. Each corner blade runs along the road it names, as real ones do, and the roadside ones face whoever's walking that way. A phone in portrait sees a narrow slice of the road, so they're big enough to read before they slide off screen.
- **The church signs:** one at every church, side-on to the road on the front lawn, and changed every day. A white cabinet with a blue header in a serif, flaking from the top down, and under it a board lit from behind by two tubes, with black changeable letters on rails: Bungee squeezed to 70% wide, each a little crooked and now and then one faded. They're measured off the owner's three photos and made bigger than life, 3 m across the face, so a phone can read them from 20 m, the way the street signs are. The base is half as wide as the board with a post at each end, in a brick planter with red flowers and kudzu. They're the brightest thing on any street, but the board stays under the bloom so the letters hold. Knocked over, they go dark. `data/signs.md` has the rest.
- **Toni's:** the burned house on the south side of Gallatin St, straight across from Clay St, a ruin with a brown roof that nobody lives in and nobody parks at. A ditch runs along the road in front of it, mud with a thin line of water that's the sky in it, rushes along both sides and a gap where the drive crosses, and tall grass grows all round the house, thinning out away from it.
- **Robby's lot:** what's left of a burned single-wide in a mown clearing in the woods off Chestnut St, at the top of a dirt drive, side-on to the road as it stood on Street View in 2014. Its steel frame, rusted and bowed, on concrete blocks you can count, with the hitch to the north on its jack. Ribbed tin walls, white below what's left of the paint and scorched black above it and round the windows, leaning out where the heat bent them or lying on the grass where they fell. The roof's in across the frame, the stove's gone through the floor, the storm door's swung out on its hinge, and block steps go up to a door that isn't there. He stands at the foot of them, with a ring of blocks in the yard to cook on. The memorial's cross and flowers and its letters don't take the night's light, so they read from the road. Robby's the one ghost: a plain pale look, and every other pixel of him gone on the same 12 fps step as the boil, in a line that glows. The flies are pale where it's dark behind them and dark where he's behind them. With reduced motion his holes hold still and the flies move at a quarter of the rate.
- **The compound on Pine Forest Rd:** from the aerial, measured. The real gravel drive runs in through the pines past the shed nearest the road, past a plain white metal building and a fenced lawn, to a yard between a long building with a rusted back roof and a big two-block house with a porch down each side and a pool in a concrete deck. Down the hill north of the house, under the trees by the river, is a plank cabin with big windows over the water and decks round it: a swing on the south one, the outdoor cinema on the one west of its door, and a little art studio at the end of the deck south of that. Nothing on the way says whose it is. It's the most lit-up place in the woods: the bulb in the shed, and the film on the screen down by the cabin. The countdown on the screen is an ordinary film leader, because a film would be somebody's.
- **The yard off True Light Rd:** from the aerial, measured. A gravel track leaves True Light Rd and runs up the edge of a field to a long low house under a red metal hip roof, L-shaped, with an oval of concrete and a bright blue pool on its east side. Across the gravel there's a big pale metal building with a red lean-to, a little cream shed, and a trampoline in the grass at the end of a white walk. The net stands in a worn patch of the yard under a light on a pole, which isn't on the aerial. The gear's the Cardinals' own: the two birds on the bat with the name under them on a red felt pennant and across a white home jersey on the wall of the little shed, StL on a red cap, a bat with red tape on the grip, a bigger pennant on the big building where it can be seen from the net, and somebody's StL cap on a post of the net. Tan pasture from the river's bank to hard edges of woods, a kilometre across and rolling, with fine tufts of grass near you, dark woven-wire fences with braced posts at the gateways running off into the fog, and big lone pines on the skyline. The barns: a long red-roofed one, a white one, a silver one and a long equipment shed under a blue-grey roof, round a yard of gravel. A white house with two gables by the farm road and a long low blue-roofed one in the trees. A pond the bright yellow-green of algae, with an island with one pine on it and a dock. Round bales lying by the bare rings where they were fed.
- **The trailer by the ball field:** long and white with dark shutters, white skirting and a stained deck at its door. It's dark inside apart from a beige computer on a laminate desk at the far end, off to one side, throwing teal on the carpet and the wall behind it. The picture on it is the only bright thing in the room. Up close it fills a phone's width and every word can be read, with lines across it, the glass bowed and the corners a little dark.

- **The gas station:** from Street View, by eye: a long, low plank store with a white metal roof, and in front of it a wide flat canopy on red posts, cream with a red band along the top, over two old pumps. There's a bench against the wall and a picnic table by the pumps. The canopy's kept clear of kudzu. Inside is invented: three rows of shelves, a counter by the door and coolers along the back. The owner says it's been shut for about ten years, so the coolers are off, and the ice box is rusted, with one door hanging open. What's on the shelves is drawn with no writing on it. What's left on the ice box says ICE, which isn't anyone's. Barring the door is boards across the doorway.
- **The church on Church St:** where Miss Bobby runs the pantry, from Street View, by eye: tan brick and steep, with white siding in its gables. Its front is the south gable, with the door in the middle under a little porch on two white posts, a step of concrete under that, and above it a round window of blue glass with a white cross in it. A white steeple stands on the ridge just behind the front, square, with a louvre in each face and a white spire, and comes down when enough of the church does. The roof and the steeple come off while he's inside. Inside is invented: dark pews either side of a red runner up the nave, the table, the pulpit and a brass cross up a step at the far end, an upright piano in the alcove, and through a door in a plastered wall halfway up, the pantry: grey shelving with boxes and bags on it and no writing on any of it, where the cans on the middle shelf are what the pantry has. What the lights would throw is warm pools on the floor. The bell rope hangs just inside the door with a red woollen grip, and gives a tug at each stroke, except with reduced motion.
- **Charlie Flake's marker:** a plaque on a pole, bigger than life like the street signs, cream on a dark blue-green, and a catfish on top side-on to the road at its true 1.2 m: flat wide head, four whiskers, a forked tail and a slate back that doesn't take the night's light. Seen from the road you're looking up at it, so its belly stays small and dull, or it reads as a whale.
- **The railroad:** the real line through town on dark ties, and a train on it every five minutes or so. One or two locomotives in faded blue-grey, brown or green, with an orange nose and black stripes on it like the warning triangle's, then a long string of boxcars, covered hoppers and tank cars in rust, grey and black, with no lettering and no railroad's colours anywhere on it. Its headlight comes first, a star down the line, then a glare, with the track lit up ahead of it, and a red light blinks on the last car. Each of the five road crossings has a grey mast either side with a crossbuck and two red lamps that take turns, a red and white arm, and a white box on one corner, as at Chestnut St. The trestles are black creosoted timber on pile bents, with nothing at the sides but a platform halfway over to stand clear on. By the west one, a plain marker for the 1863 wreck. `data/railroad.md` has the rest.
- **What he throws:** an empty brown bottle with a scrap of paper label, a red brick of firecrackers and a yellow tin of lighter fluid. The cans of beans he carries aren't thrown any more. Whatever's lying around has a small warm glint, so that it can be found at night, and it's all one draw call. A bottle isn't his bottle: his has the cyan and the black label, and nothing else in the game does. Firecrackers spit sparks and flash on the twelves. A car alarm flashes its lights, front and back, or holds them on with reduced motion.
- **The hills:** the real ones, at their true height. There aren't a lot, but they're prominent: the land's nearly flat round town and along the river, then US 80 goes up 25 m in a third of a mile and the cemetery up Chunky Duffee Rd is about 49 m over town's floodplain. A road goes up and over, and the far side of a rise is hidden until he's on it. The ground's flat-shaded, so a slope shades itself. See `data/hills.md`.
- **The land past the fog:** the same land with far fewer points, so there's a low dark line of far hills under the sky from anywhere in town, in the fog's colour going towards the horizon's. It isn't in the first scene.
- **The river:** it takes glowing rings where things hit it. Cottonmouths open white mouths before striking. Whoever they've had comes up again a little way downstream, on the bank, with one hanging off a sleeve.
- **The ponds:** the same water as the river, with the sky and the moon in it, each at its own height just under its lowest bank. Dark mud at the edge, and the far bank and its trees black against the sky.
- **The lantern:** the one from the local legend, a guide light with nobody holding it that leads him across the river.
- **The light on Stuckey's Bridge:** a cold, pale point with a soft blue halo and no lamp around it, which only comes after the first chapter. It steps on the twelves like everything else, and it borrows the lantern's light, because one more in the scene would cost every material on a phone. `docs/lore.md` has what it's from.

The brief is a "perfectly 3d modeled city": the real layout and landmarks of Chunky on real street geometry, rendered in this hand-drawn style. Accuracy applies to where things are and what they are. It doesn't mean photorealism. The roads, the railroad and the river come from the road graph, and the buildings from `data/structures.json`.

## The bite and the fever

Between the end of the bridge run and the edge of town there isn't a word, and nothing to press. What's in the dream is the owner's to decide, so for now it's only a look.

- **The moccasin.** It's lying where the boards stop. It rears up in an S with its mouth wide open, and it strikes on the 12 fps step. The camera cuts low and close, side on, so that a black shape with a white mouth stands against the sky. It gets its own head for this, a flat spade that opens right up, and it's drawn about twice the size of the ones in the water, because the one moment you see it should read.
- **He goes down.** The hit is a glitch and a flash, then he staggers and falls on the 12 fps step while the camera swings up and away.
- **The fever.** Hot pink and cyan take over the palette, the picture warps in rows and smears through the last frame, and the camera rises off him and out along the road towards a horizon that won't hold still. It fades to black.
- **He comes to.** Face down on the planks of the Chunky River Bridge, with the fever still in the sky. It takes a few seconds more to leave him.
- **Reduced motion.** The same scene with a still camera: the tint and the fade, no warping, no shake and no glitch, in under half the time.

### When a try ends

Every death is that fever, full of whatever got him, and there isn't a word in any of it. He goes down on the 12 fps step, the fever comes up, and about five seconds after the hit he's playing again where he starts, with the fever still in the sky and his heart the first thing heard in the dark. A tap, a click or a key goes straight to him coming to. `src/fever.js` has when each part happens.

- **The crowd.** They stand round him in a ring, folded over and looking down, glowing hotter than anything else in the game. The camera's where he lies, looking straight up through them, wide enough that the ring's there on a phone stood on end, and turning slowly. None of him is drawn, since from there he'd be the inside of his hood. It's warped half as much, because it's close.
- **The train.** The camera rises off him beside the nearest car. The boxcars keep going by and stretch to three times their length, and the train's light swells from where its lamp is until it's nearly all there is.
- **A wreck.** Whatever on wheels hit him goes up off him and over, end over end, slowly, the way it was going. It's seen side on against a pale sky, from low by where he's lying, and the horizon rocks. When he comes to, it's back where it was.
- **A blast, a fire, or something heavy coming down.** The flash becomes the sky: pale pink for a blast, orange for a fire, dust for what came down. The camera rises off him from the far side of what got him, looking at it and up into the smoke coming off it, which the fever bends into rows.
- **The cottonmouths.** The bite's, rising straight up off him, looking down and turning.
- **The river.** He goes under, and his hood comes up once while the camera comes up out of the water over where he went in. On the bridge run he comes to at the start of it, and the run starts again.
- **Reduced motion.** The same scenes held still: no rising or turning, no stretching, no tumbling and no rocking, with him down from the start.

## Sound

The night comes first: crickets one here and one there with quiet between them, the river, and frogs near water, all of it thin enough that a footstep carries. Nothing under it is a loop or a hiss. The music is slow and has a lot of room in it, a low drone and a slide guitar a long way off, and it only gets a pulse when something's after him.

- **The rope snapping is the loudest silence in the game.** The music cuts as it goes, so there's only air and then the splash, and it comes back under the water.
- **The bridge** gets drums and a pulse. **The bite** cuts everything dead. **The fever** takes the night away altogether and leaves the music going out of tune. **Waking** is his heart.
- **The crowd** mutter to themselves. One that's noticed him says so, louder the closer it is, and the music turns on him until they've been gone a while. One that reaches for him groans once more as its hands go up, higher and shorter, since by then his hood is hiding it.
- **The train** is heard before it's seen: a rumble from over a kilometre off, higher as it comes and lower as it goes, the horn once, long, long, short, long, short of a crossing he's near, the crossing bell, and the wheels over the joints up close. Two short blasts mean it's seen him on the tracks.
- **A car alarm** whoops, blares and warbles for half a minute, the kind every car had, and never a siren, since Chunky has no police.
- The effects and the night noise each have a slider of their own, under the button beside the mute.
- **Nothing speaks for him.** His voice is the owner's to record. There's a place for each line, and no line exists until there's a recording of it.

`data/sound.md` has how it's all kept.

## What only the reference has

The game has the reference's clean character, holds, timed bursts, full bottle, drawstrings and particles. These stay in the reference on purpose:

- **Mirror reflections.** The reference renders a `Reflector`. The game's water fakes the sky and moon, which is what the trailer shows and what a phone can afford.
- **The drink.** In the reference he raises and tips the bottle on a loop, with his hood bent back to meet it. In the game he holds it at his side until he decides to live, as in the trailer.

## Type and words

- Bungee for titles, prompts, names and buttons. IBM Plex Mono for everything else.
- Keep words sparse outside dialogue. The reference scene shows only its name, by design.
- Any words that appear must be readable, and nothing covers them. On a phone the box of words at the bottom goes up over whichever buttons it would meet.
- Nothing on the screen says why something happened or how something works, or tells him what he can already see. Sound, light and the way people move do that. Words on the screen are for what people say, what he picks up, the controls and where to go next. What he picks up and the bean count have nothing after them: "+1 gas can", "-1 beans".

## Motion

- Each frame should be structurally close to the last, with no sudden jumps in direction or shape.
- Motion shouldn't be so subtle that nothing but one prop seems alive.
- Check new animation for deformities and sudden transitions before calling it done.

## Controls

Touch comes first, because the game is played on a phone. The left thumb moves and dragging looks around. The buttons say what they do right now, like CUT, TALK, GET IN, SWING, THROW, JUMP, RUN, BRAKE and SWIM FAST, and a button with nothing to do isn't there. SHAMBLE is the one that stays on: dark with a cream ring when it's off, and solid cream while he's passing for one of them. Driving takes one thumb: forward on the stick is the gas, off to the side steers and keeps the gas on, and back brakes and reverses. On a computer it plays like any third-person game: moving the mouse looks around with nothing held, WASD or arrows move with the camera and A and D go sideways, Shift runs, Space jumps, swims faster or is the handbrake, E uses, a click swings at what's in the middle of the screen, a right click throws there, the wheel changes what's in his hand, C shambles and M is the map. On foot the camera stays where it's put, and in something with an engine it comes back behind it after a moment. A click takes the mouse, Esc lets it go, and talking, the map and the cards let it go and take it back after. While someone's talking in their own words, any key but an answer's number starts him typing to them, and Enter says it. Where the page can't take the mouse, it looks around all the same and keeps turning at the sides of the page. A strip at the bottom shows what works right now. `src/controls.js` has the lists.

## What stays out of this repo

- **Music without rights.** The existing video captures of the bridge scene are cut to a commercial track with no licence, so neither they nor the track belong here.
- **Google's imagery.** Street View and the satellite are the source of truth for where things are and what they look like. Roads start from Census TIGER/Line data and buildings from USA Structures, and they're corrected wherever Street View and the satellite show something else, but none of the pictures go in the repo.
- **Reference images,** until the owner decides they can be public.
